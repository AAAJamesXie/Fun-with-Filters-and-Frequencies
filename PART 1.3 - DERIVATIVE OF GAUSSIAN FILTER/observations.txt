I selected sigma = 2 and T = 0.04. Gaussian smoothing suppresses the dense grass responses seen with raw finite differences and produces smoother, wider responses around the photographer and tripod. At T = 0.02, the edge map retains more facial and background detail but also has thicker contours and additional clutter. T = 0.04 preserves much of the shoulder, face/camera structure and tripod while removing most fine grass texture. At T = 0.06, more weak camera and background contours disappear; T = 0.08 and 0.10 fragment the tripod and lose much of the subject detail. Some thin and low-contrast edges are lost even at the selected threshold. This is not a perfect contour detector: smoothing trades spatial detail for lower texture sensitivity, and thresholding cannot classify semantic edges. The sigma sweep holds the threshold fixed to make the scale change explicit; edge density alone does not establish which scale is best.
